Multi-Component VAE with Gaussian Markov Random Field

Fouad Oubari1,2,  Mohamed El Baha3,  Raphaël Meunier2,  Rodrigue Décatoire2,  Mathilde Mougeot1,3
1Université Paris-Saclay, CNRS, ENS Paris-Saclay, Centre Borelli   2Michelin   3ENSIIE
Paper arXiv Code

Conditional generation on the BIKED dataset. Given one bicycle component (left), our model generates all remaining parts while preserving structural coherence.

Abstract

Multi-component datasets with intricate dependencies challenge current generative modeling techniques. Existing Multi-component Variational AutoEncoders rely on simplified aggregation strategies — such as Product of Experts (PoE) or Mixture of Experts (MoE) — that compromise structural coherence across generated components by assuming independence in the latent space.

We introduce the Gaussian Markov Random Field Multi-Component VAE (GMRF MCVAE), embedding Gaussian Markov Random Fields into both prior and posterior distributions to explicitly model cross-component relationships. This enables richer representation and faithful reproduction of complex interactions without requiring additional decoder passes or approximations at inference time.

Empirically, our model achieves state-of-the-art performance on a synthetic Copula dataset designed for intricate component relationships, competitive results on PolyMNIST, and significantly enhanced structural coherence on the real-world BIKED bicycle dataset.

The Problem with Existing MCVAEs

Standard multi-component VAEs encode each component separately, then aggregate the latent representations using PoE or MoE strategies. These approaches implicitly assume that components are independent in latent space — an assumption that breaks down for structured data like bicycle assemblies, CT-MRI pairs, or correlated financial markets.

Concretely, this leads to three known failure modes:

  • Veto effect (PoE): overconfident encoders dominate the joint posterior, ignoring other components.
  • Averaging effect (MoE / MMVAE): encodings are biased towards information common to all components, losing diversity.
  • Exponential cost (MoPoE): evaluating all 2M−1 subsets is intractable for large M.

Our solution: embed a Gaussian Markov Random Field directly in the latent space so that cross-component covariances are learned and enforced during both training and generation.

Method

🔗 Structured latent space

The joint latent z = (z₁,…,z_M) follows a multivariate Gaussian with a full block covariance Σ = [Σᵢⱼ]. Diagonal blocks Σᵢᵢ come from component encoders; off-diagonal blocks Σᵢⱼ come from a shared global encoder, explicitly encoding cross-component correlations.

✅ SPD guarantee (Theorem 1)

Off-diagonal blocks are scaled by √(αᵢαⱼ) to enforce block diagonal dominance. We prove this guarantees symmetric positive definiteness — enabling valid Cholesky sampling via the reparameterization trick with no approximation.

⚡ Closed-form conditioning

Conditioning on observed component zⱼ yields an exact Gaussian via the Schur complement: p(zᵢ | zⱼ) = 𝒩(μᵢ + Σᵢⱼ Σⱼⱼ⁻¹(zⱼ − μⱼ), Σᵢᵢ − Σᵢⱼ Σⱼⱼ⁻¹ Σⱼᵢ). No additional training or approximate inference needed.

🚀 Computational efficiency

Baselines need O(M²) decoder passes for cross-reconstruction. GMRF MCVAE replaces these with a closed-form matrix operation — O(M²d²) covariance build + O(M) self-decodes. In practice: 128 s/epoch vs 350 s for MVAE on PolyMNIST (same GPU).

Key contributions

  • Novel architecture embedding GMRFs in both prior and posterior of a Multi-Component VAE.
  • Practical SPD construction via blockwise assembly with a proven diagonal dominance guarantee.
  • New metric for structural coherence — Wasserstein distance between centers of mass of generated components, applicable to any multi-part industrial dataset.
  • State-of-the-art on synthetic Copula, competitive on PolyMNIST, best structural coherence on BIKED.

Experiments

Copula Dataset — dependency modeling

Synthetic benchmark with four 2D components generated via Gaussian Copulas with high cross-component correlations (|ρ| = 0.9). Each component marginal is uniform U([0,1]), so the only challenge is modeling the joint dependency structure. We measure Wasserstein distance between generated and true joint distributions.

BIKED — real-world structural coherence

4,500 bicycle designs with five components (saddle, frame, crank, wheels, handlebars). The most demanding benchmark: generated parts must spatially assemble into physically plausible bikes. We introduce a new metric — Wasserstein distance between component centers of mass — to quantify spatial coherence without relying on hand-crafted geometric rules.
